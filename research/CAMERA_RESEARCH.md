## 1. Ideas / Opportunities
_Candidate features or improvements, one bullet each, with a one-line rationale._

**Primary use case (2026-08-17): shooting high-quality photo + video from far range at concerts** — dim/mixed stage lighting, heavy digital zoom, crowd jostling (larger/lower-frequency motion than casual hand shake). This reprioritizes the features below.

- **Super Resolution** (primary focus) — multi-frame burst capture + alignment + merge, then Core ML super-res model for detail hallucination. See §3 for pipeline.
- **Night mode / multi-frame denoising** (elevated to co-primary, was 3rd) — shares burst-capture + alignment + merge infrastructure with Super Resolution. This is likely the bigger contributor to "less grainy than iPhone" at concerts specifically, since low/mixed stage lighting is the exact condition that causes grain — build in parallel with Super Resolution rather than after it.
- **Zoom Lock** (primary focus, video-first) — gyro + Vision-based crop stabilization on top of standard digital zoom. User's stated use case is video, not just photos, so the video/continuous-stream stabilization path is core scope, not a later extension. Needs a wider correction range than casual hand-shake tuning to handle crowd jostling.
- Auto Framing (video) — natural extension of Zoom Lock into video via Vision object tracking; lower priority.

## 2. Competitor & Prior Art
_What other camera apps (stock iOS Camera, Halide, VSCO, Obscura, etc.) do well or poorly._

- **Camac** (iOS) — reportedly built a custom "Super Resolution RAW" mode: multi-frame capture, sub-pixel shift analysis across frames to eliminate noise, then NN/SRGAN-based upscaling. Validates that a custom SR pipeline is feasible on iOS hardware without needing Apple's built-in Deep Fusion.
- Samsung Camera app — see feature breakdown in conversation (Nightography, Scene Optimizer, Auto Framing, Expert RAW, Single Take). Super Resolution + Zoom Lock chosen as the two flagship features to replicate first.
- **Leica-tuned apps** (Xiaomi/Sharp Aquos co-branded software, Leica FOTOS/Lux) — opposite philosophy from Samsung: minimal processing, "Leica Authentic" profile aims for true-to-life color/dynamic range rather than algorithmic punch, vs. a more restrained "Leica Vibrant" profile. Manual controls surfaced more prominently. Relevant to us only as a possible finishing-pass style toggle (sharpen strength, tone-curve lift, ML blend weight) on top of the same Core pipeline — not a different pipeline. Not in scope now since the stated goal is replicating Samsung's clarity/low-grain specifically, but worth keeping in mind if a "natural" look option is wanted later (see Open Questions).
- **[ImageStackAlignator](https://github.com/kunzmi/ImageStackAlignator)** — C#/CUDA reimplementation of Google's Handheld Multi-Frame Super-Resolution paper (Pixel 3/4 Super Res Zoom). Reference implementation for our exact pipeline shape (align → merge → upscale), with three concrete upgrade paths worth revisiting once our naive v1 works:
  - **Alignment upgrade:** global cross-correlation + multi-scale patch tracking + sequential-frame outlier rejection, vs. our current pure-translation `VNTranslationalImageRegistrationRequest`. Worth reevaluating `VNHomographicImageRegistrationRequest` for this.
  - **Merge upgrade:** structure-tensor-adaptive reconstruction kernels (treats edges vs. flat regions differently) vs. our flat confidence-weighted average.
  - **Real optical super-resolution:** resampling multiple sub-pixel-shifted low-res frames onto a higher-res grid gives genuine extra resolution, not ML hallucination — a legitimate "Stage 1.5" to consider before/alongside the pretrained-model ML upscale path.
- Checked [multi-camera image combination article](https://medium.com/aimonks/combining-images-from-multiple-cameras-methods-deep-learning-techniques-and-practical-77e192e3210e) — not directly applicable (it covers panorama stitching/photogrammetry/NeRF for spatially-different viewpoints, not our temporally-sequential same-viewpoint burst problem). One transferable piece: **SIFT/SURF/ORB feature detection** from the classical stitching pipeline is a viable alternative to Vision's registration requests if we ever need to fuse simultaneous shots from *different lenses* (main + telephoto) — a genuinely different problem from burst merge, relevant only if we pursue true multi-lens zoom fusion later.
- **Lux (makers of Halide/Kino/Spectre)** — the most credible third-party iOS camera dev team; checked two of their technical blog posts. Their actual algorithms are proprietary/undisclosed (as expected — that's their product moat), but two concrete, verified takeaways:
  - [The Making of Kino](https://www.lux.camera/kino-a-pro-video-camera-in-four-months/) — direct quote: *"on the iPhone 15 Pro it isn't possible to shoot: 1) Apple Log video; 2) at 60 FPS; 3) with stabilization enabled; 4) when shooting from the telephoto camera"* — an undocumented format/stabilization/frame-rate incompatibility they only discovered through testing, causing a "dead viewfinder" (stream shuts down on invalid combos). Also: their AutoMotion (cinematic exposure) algorithm actively conflicted with stabilization, and even with 100 beta testers, the public release surfaced more bugs "usually when recording at 60 frames per second" on older devices. **Confirms this class of bug (silent failures on specific format/stabilization/frame-rate combinations) is a known, expected pain point industry-wide** — not something we did unusually wrong; even a well-resourced team ships it and finds more via wide release. Validates our own approach of empirically restricting risky paths (e.g. our own manual-format-vs-preset split) rather than trusting AVFoundation's documented behavior alone.
  - [Beyond Spectre 1.0](https://www.lux.camera/beyond-spectre-1-0/) (multi-frame long-exposure merge, closest analog to our Super Res/Night Mode) — confirms dropped/misaligned frames directly cause visible merge artifacts (their case: gaps in light trails; our case: ghosting), validating our confidence-threshold frame-rejection in `FrameAligner`. Also ships **device-tiered processing** (different algorithm behavior on iPhone 7/SE vs. newer hardware) — something we don't do at all; our burst frame counts/pipeline are currently fixed regardless of device capability. Worth revisiting if we ever need to support older/weaker hardware well.
  - **Bottom line: there is no publicly available "correct algorithm" to copy from Lux or anyone else for this space** — the actual stabilization/merge math is closed-source. What's learnable from them is process (test widely, expect undocumented hardware gotchas, tier by device) not algorithms. We're on our own for the actual computational-photography logic, consistent with the two-stage classical-then-ML plan already in this file.

## 3. Technical Findings
_AVFoundation/Camera capture APIs, hardware capabilities, performance notes, gotchas._

### Shared burst pipeline (feeds Super Resolution + Night Mode)
1. **Capture** — burst N frames (8–20) via `AVCapturePhotoOutput` (multi-lens fusion: `isVirtualDeviceConstituentPhotoDeliveryEnabled`) or `AVCaptureVideoDataOutput`.
2. **Align** — `Vision` frame registration to compute sub-pixel alignment between frames caused by natural hand shake. **Implemented 2026-08-17** in `FrameAligner.swift`: tries `VNHomographicImageRegistrationRequest` first (Vision's built-in feature-matching + outlier-rejection, fits a full perspective warp — corrects rotation/perspective, not just translation), falls back to `VNTranslationalImageRegistrationRequest` when homographic registration fails (typically low-texture/low-feature scenes without enough distinctive features to match). Warping now happens inside `FrameAligner` (via `CIPerspectiveTransform` for the homographic case), not `FrameMerger` — the two registration methods produce different transform types (3x3 perspective warp vs. `CGAffineTransform`), so `AlignedFrame` now carries an already-warped `CIImage` instead of a separate transform, keeping `FrameMerger` transform-agnostic.
3. **Merge** — Metal compute kernel / `CIKernel`, weighted average or median merge onto a supersampled grid; per-pixel confidence/outlier masking to reject ghosting from motion.
4. **Detail/finish pass** — this is where Super Resolution and Night Mode diverge (see below).

### Algorithm survey (2026-08-17)
Named algorithms relevant to each piece of this pipeline, for reference when picking upgrades:
- **Alignment:** phase correlation (what `VNTranslationalImageRegistrationRequest` approximates), Lucas-Kanade optical flow, ORB/SIFT/FAST feature matching + RANSAC (what `VNHomographicImageRegistrationRequest` does internally — now in use, see above).
- **Merge/denoise:** weighted averaging (current), structure-tensor-adaptive merging (edge- vs. flat-region-aware — next upgrade candidate per ImageStackAlignator), non-local means, Wiener filtering.
- **Super-resolution:** iterative back-projection / sub-pixel-shift resampling (classical, genuine optical SR — **implemented 2026-08-17** as Stage 1.5, see below), ESRGAN/Real-ESRGAN/SwinIR (ML, Stage 2 — not yet built), kernel regression / adaptive kernel reconstruction (the actual technique behind Google's Handheld Multi-Frame Super-Resolution paper — a further refinement beyond our simpler per-frame resampling approach).
- **Zoom Lock:** complementary filter (current, in `MotionProvider`), Kalman filter (principled replacement for hand-tuned complementary-filter constants — recommended next upgrade, see Open Questions), Extended/Unscented Kalman filter (needed if fusing gyro with Vision feature tracking, nonlinear), motion-vector-smoothing (extract per-frame motion, smooth the camera-path trajectory, crop to follow the smoothed path).
- **Tone mapping:** global tone curve (current, in `FrameFinisher`), local Laplacian filters (edge-aware, region-adaptive), Retinex-based decomposition (illumination vs. reflectance, lifts shadows without uniformly boosting noise).

### Super Resolution — build plan
- **Stage 1 (classical, done):** align/merge + `CIUnsharpMask` sharpening finish. Validated the pipeline but was *not actually super-resolution* — it merged/denoised/sharpened at native input resolution, never increasing pixel count.
- **Stage 1.5 (classical optical upscale, done 2026-08-17):** `FrameAligner.align(outputScale:)` now resamples each burst frame's *sub-pixel* alignment offset (Vision reports fractional-pixel translation/warp, not whole-pixel) onto a grid `outputScale`× the native size — Super Resolution mode uses 2×. Because different frames land at different sub-pixel positions on the larger canvas, the combined result resolves real detail a single frame's native pixel grid couldn't — this is genuine optical super-resolution (iterative-back-projection-family classical SR), not just upscaling one frame's existing pixels. Night Mode stays at `outputScale = 1` (native) since its goal is denoising/exposure recovery, not added resolution. Implementation: the scale is composed into each frame's alignment transform *before* the single resampling operation (not applied as a second separate upscale pass afterward) — doing it as one combined resample is what preserves the sub-pixel information; upscaling after the fact would just interpolate already-resolved native-resolution data with no new detail.
- **Stage 2 (ML upscale, not yet built):** replace/augment the classical finishing pass with a pretrained Core ML super-resolution model run via `VNCoreMLRequest`/`MLModel` on the merged frame. This mirrors what Camac reportedly does (multi-frame alignment + noise elimination, then NN-based upscaling) — classical alignment/merge is the same regardless of ML vs non-ML finish. With Stage 1.5 now producing a real 2×-resolution merged frame, Stage 2's model would refine/hallucinate additional detail on top of genuine optical upscaling rather than needing to invent all the added resolution itself.
- Training a custom SRGAN from scratch is not realistic (needs paired LR/HR dataset + real GPU compute). Practical path: take a pretrained model, convert with `coremltools`, optionally fine-tune later.
- **Candidate pretrained models to evaluate for Core ML conversion:**
  - **Real-ESRGAN** — strong general-purpose SR, widely used baseline, active community Core ML conversions exist.
  - **SwinIR** — transformer-based, higher quality but heavier; check Neural Engine inference time before committing.
  - **ESPCN / FSRCNN** — much lighter, classic CNN-based SR; good fallback if Real-ESRGAN/SwinIR are too slow on-device, weaker detail hallucination.
  - Apple's own `coremltools` has a super-resolution conversion example worth checking as a starting template.
- **Decision point:** benchmark Neural Engine inference time for each candidate on merged burst output before locking one in as default; keep classical Stage 1 pass as a fallback path if ML inference is too slow for interactive use.

### Night Mode
- Same shared pipeline (steps 1–3), but exposure/ISO tuned per frame for low light (short exposure to avoid blur, frame count compensates via ~√N SNR improvement).
- Finish with local tone mapping (`CIToneCurve`/custom Metal) instead of SR upscale.

### Zoom Lock
- Not a burst-capture feature — real-time crop stabilization layer on `videoZoomFactor`.
- Capture wider FOV than requested zoom, use `CMMotionManager` (gyro, low-latency) fused with `Vision` feature tracking to compute a smoothed crop offset that cancels hand shake.
- Complementary/Kalman filter needed to smooth the correction signal — raw gyro alone causes jitter/overshoot.
- **Concert use case:** motion source is not just hand tremor — standing in a packed crowd, on toes, getting bumped. Needs a larger correction window/digital-crop margin than casual-use tuning, and the smoothing filter's cutoff likely needs retuning for lower-frequency, larger-amplitude motion.
- **Video is core scope, not an extension** — the continuous-stream stabilization path (real-time per-frame crop correction) needs to be built alongside/before the photo-burst-only pipeline, since the primary use case is video.

## 4. Open Questions
_Things to investigate further._

- Which pretrained SR model (Real-ESRGAN vs SwinIR vs ESPCN/FSRCNN) hits acceptable Neural Engine inference latency on target device(s)?
- Does `coremltools` conversion of Real-ESRGAN/SwinIR preserve quality, or does quantization for on-device speed degrade output too much?
- Is a small fine-tuning dataset needed to get a Samsung-like "look," or is a stock pretrained model good enough for v1?
- How many burst frames is the right tradeoff between alignment/merge quality and capture latency (shutter lag)?
- Would a "Leica Authentic"-style gentler finishing pass (less sharpening/tone lift) be worth offering as an alternate style once the Samsung-style pipeline works, given both would reuse the same Core pipeline?
- What crop margin/correction window does Zoom Lock need for crowd-jostling motion vs. casual hand shake — needs real testing at a live event or a simulated equivalent.
- Does homographic (feature-matching) alignment actually reduce blur/ghosting vs. the old translation-only approach on real burst photos? Needs device testing with real content, especially high-contrast scenes and any hand rotation during the burst.
- Kalman filter for Zoom Lock (replacing the hand-tuned complementary filter in `MotionProvider`) — next recommended upgrade per the algorithm survey above; would remove the manual constant-tuning cycle we went through (sensitivity/smoothing/recenterRate) in favor of a principled, self-tuning fusion of gyro + (eventually) visual tracking.

## 4.5 AVFoundation API Survey (2026-08-18)
_What Apple's own capture APIs already offer that we could lean on instead of building from scratch._

- **`AVCaptureVideoStabilizationMode`** — built-in gyro+crop stabilization, set via `AVCaptureConnection.preferredVideoStabilizationMode`. Tiers: `.standard`/`.cinematic` (baseline), `.cinematicExtended` (wider correction margin), `.cinematicExtendedEnhanced` (newest/strongest). This is functionally the same problem `ZoomLockController`/`MotionProvider`/`KalmanFilter1D` solve by hand — worth A/B testing before investing further in the custom crowd-jostling margin tuning.
- **No built-in multi-frame video denoise API exists.** `automaticallyEnablesLowLightBoostWhenAvailable` is single-frame exposure/gain boost, not multi-frame temporal merge, and is reportedly unreliable/format-restricted per developer forum reports. Confirms the custom `FrameMerger`-based approach is still required for video temporal denoising — nothing to pull from Apple here.
- **`AVCaptureSmartFramingMonitor`** (iOS 26) — real API for gyro-free face/gaze-based auto-framing (aspect ratio + zoom recommendations), but front-camera-only (`.builtInUltraWideCamera`, iPhone 17/Air/17 Pro) and photo-format-only. Not applicable to the rear-camera concert-video use case; keep in mind only if front-camera "Auto Framing" is ever pursued.
- **Known risk:** developer forum reports describe an audio/video sync bug (~1-1.5s lag) when combining `cinematicExtended` stabilization with `AVCaptureVideoDataOutput` + `AVAssetWriter` specifically (not reported with `AVCaptureMovieFileOutput`) — exactly our `VideoRecorder` architecture. Must verify on real device before trusting `.cinematicExtended` for recorded (not just previewed) video.
- **Implemented 2026-08-18:** added `VideoStabilizationStrategy` (`.custom` / `.cinematicExtended`) to `CameraSessionController`, exposed as a toggle in the video settings panel (`CameraView.swift`, "STABILIZATION" row). `.cinematicExtended` sets `preferredVideoStabilizationMode` on the video connection and disables the custom `ZoomLockController` crop (`zoomLock.isEnabled = false`) so the two paths don't stack. `.custom` explicitly sets `preferredVideoStabilizationMode = .off` so it's a clean comparison against AVFoundation's default rather than stacking on top of it.

## 5. Decisions Log
_Date — decision — why._

- 2026-08-17 — Chose Super Resolution and Zoom Lock as the two primary features to replicate first, with Night Mode as a natural third once shared burst-capture/alignment/merge infrastructure exists. Why: these are Samsung's flagship differentiators and share enough underlying pipeline (burst + alignment + merge) to be built together efficiently.
- 2026-08-17 — Super Resolution will be built in two stages: classical alignment/merge/sharpen first, then swap in a pretrained Core ML SR model (Real-ESRGAN/SwinIR/ESPCN candidates) as the finishing pass. Why: training a custom SRGAN from scratch isn't realistic without a paired dataset and GPU compute; a pretrained-model swap gets the same "multi-frame + NN upscale" result (as Camac reportedly does) without that cost, and keeps a working fallback if ML inference is too slow on-device.
- 2026-08-17 — Reprioritized: primary use case is far-range photo/video at concerts (dim/mixed lighting, heavy zoom, crowd jostling). Night mode's denoising elevated to co-primary with Super Resolution (build in parallel, not after) since low-light grain reduction — not just detail upscaling — is likely the bigger driver of "less grainy than iPhone." Zoom Lock's video/continuous-stream stabilization is now core scope, not a later extension, since the user's stated goal is video specifically. Why: original priority order (Super Res → Zoom Lock → Night Mode as afterthought) didn't match the actual target use case.
- 2026-08-17 — Upgraded `FrameAligner` from translation-only to homographic (feature-matching) registration via `VNHomographicImageRegistrationRequest`, with translational as a fallback for low-feature scenes. Why: translation-only alignment left residual blur/ghosting whenever hand rotation (not just shake) occurred during a burst — Vision's homographic registration corrects rotation/perspective too, using built-in feature matching + outlier rejection, without us needing to implement ORB/RANSAC ourselves. Warping moved from `FrameMerger` into `FrameAligner` as part of this change, since the two registration methods produce different transform types (perspective warp vs. affine) — `AlignedFrame` now carries a pre-warped image instead of a raw transform, keeping `FrameMerger` transform-agnostic.
- 2026-08-18 — Replaced `MotionProvider`'s hand-tuned complementary filter (interacting `smoothing`/`recenterRate` constants that needed several rounds of manual retuning) with a proper per-axis `KalmanFilter1D` — position modeled as mean-reverting, gyro rotation rate fed in as a velocity measurement. Why: a Kalman filter separates concerns into physically meaningful, independently-tunable parameters (process noise vs. measurement noise) and computes the optimal blend mathematically each tick, rather than a fixed hand-picked blend ratio prone to the same class of bug (sustained motion accumulating faster than it decays) resurfacing under different real-world shake characteristics.
- 2026-08-18 — Added structure-tensor-*approximate* adaptive merging to `FrameMerger`: a Sobel-based edge mask (Core Image's built-in `CIEdges`) blends the confidence-weighted average (flat/noisy regions) against the sharp reference frame (edges) per-pixel. Why: flat averaging softens edges since burst frames are only aligned up to Vision's registration accuracy — residual sub-pixel misalignment shows as slightly doubled/blurred edges once averaged. Explicitly an approximation of the real technique (ImageStackAlignator's per-pixel gradient structure tensor via a custom kernel), not the full thing — revisit with a real Metal kernel if this doesn't hold up visually.
- 2026-08-18 — Implemented Stage 1.5 of the Super Resolution plan: `FrameAligner.align(outputScale:)` composes a 2× scale into each frame's sub-pixel alignment transform before the single resampling operation, producing genuine optical super-resolution instead of same-resolution merge+sharpen. Why: "Super Resolution" mode wasn't actually increasing resolution before this — it was denoise+sharpen at native pixel count, a real correctness gap relative to what the mode is named/expected to do. Night Mode intentionally stays at native resolution (`outputScale = 1`) since its goal is denoising/exposure recovery, not added detail.
- 2026-08-18 — Added an A/B toggle between the custom Zoom Lock pipeline and AVFoundation's built-in `.cinematicExtended` stabilization (see §4.5), rather than committing further custom-tuning effort before checking if Apple's own wider-margin stabilization tier already handles concert crowd-jostling well enough. Why: cheap to test, and resolves the "what crop margin does crowd jostling need" open question directly — if `.cinematicExtended` performs well, it removes the need to keep hand-tuning `KalmanFilter1D`/`captureMargin`; if not, it's confirmation the custom path is worth the investment.
- 2026-08-18 — On-device A/B testing found no noticeable difference between `.custom` and `.cinematicExtended` in casual testing so far — inconclusive, not a verdict; needs a real concert/crowd-jostling test to actually differentiate them, since casual hand-shake is within both paths' comfort zone.
- 2026-08-18 — Added a right-hand vertical zoom gauge (log-scaled track showing live position within the device's min...max zoom range, plus the auto-engage band) and Samsung-style hold-still auto-engage for Zoom Lock (`ZoomLockController.updateAutoEngage`: ≥8x zoom + <6pt of shake for 2.5s auto-enables Zoom Lock). Why: matches a real, documented Samsung behavior (their Zoom Lock reportedly auto-triggers around 20x held for ~1.5s); our threshold is lower since the concert use case leans on Zoom Lock well below 20x and our zoom ceiling is capped at 30x rather than Samsung's higher digital range. Gyro monitoring now runs continuously (not just while Zoom Lock is enabled) so stillness can be detected *before* engaging, not just smoothed after — the manual toggle button was changed to `toggleManually()` so a deliberate user "off" isn't immediately re-triggered by auto-engage while still zoomed in.
- 2026-08-18 — Found and fixed two rounds of the same underlying bug: `CameraView` holds `@ObservedObject var zoomLock`, so *any* `@Published` change on it (e.g. `normalizedOffset`, updated every tick while Zoom Lock is active to drive the live preview's crop transform) re-invalidates the *entire* view body, including unrelated controls like the Photo/Video tab picker — enough churn makes UIKit-backed controls (the segmented `Picker`) stop registering taps. Round 1: continuous gyro monitoring (added for auto-engage) meant this 60fps churn now ran constantly even with Zoom Lock *off*, not just while a user had manually enabled it — fixed by only republishing `normalizedOffset` when it actually changes (`AESPO/Core/ZoomLock/ZoomLockController.swift`). Round 2: once auto-engage turns Zoom Lock *on*, nothing turned it back off, so the same churn resumed indefinitely after a shot (video or photo) finished, right when the user would want to switch tabs — fixed by adding `disengageAutoLock()`, called from `CameraViewModel` when a recording stops or a photo capture completes, which releases Zoom Lock only if it was auto-engaged (not manually enabled). Together these confirm hold-to-lock needs to behave as a "for this shot" aid, not a persistent mode, both for UX (matches Samsung) and for this specific rendering-churn reason.
- 2026-08-18 — Migrated `ZoomLockController`, `CameraSessionController`, and `CameraViewModel` from `ObservableObject`/`@Published`/`@StateObject`/`@ObservedObject` to Swift's `@Observable` macro (Observation framework), and did the real fix for the fragility flagged in the entry above rather than leaving it as a latent risk. Why: `ObservableObject` tracks dependencies per-*object* — any `@Published` change invalidates every view holding that object, whether or not the view's body actually reads the changed property. `@Observable` tracks per-*property*: a view only re-renders for properties it actually read in its last body evaluation. This directly targets the root cause of both bugs above (unrelated UI like the tab picker getting dragged along by `normalizedOffset` churn it never reads) rather than just working around the symptom.
  - One functional consequence required a real code change, not just a mechanical swap: `CameraPreviewView`'s live stabilized preview used to update by relying on `CameraView`'s `@ObservedObject`-driven re-renders to re-invoke `updateUIView` at 60fps and re-read `cropTransform`. Under `@Observable`, `CameraView`'s body doesn't read `normalizedOffset` directly, so that implicit 60fps trigger goes away — correctly, since the churn was the bug, but the live preview needs an intentional replacement now. Fixed by giving `CameraPreviewView.PreviewUIView` its own `CADisplayLink` that polls `zoomLock.cropTransform(forFrameSize:)` directly and applies it to the preview layer, independent of SwiftUI's render cycle entirely — the same pattern `VideoRecorder` already used for the recorded output, just now applied to the live preview too. `updateUIView` still sets the transform once synchronously for correctness on first layout.
  - Also hit a real `@Observable` macro limitation: it doesn't support `lazy var` (its synthesized init accessors only handle directly-initialized stored properties), and a stored property's default-value expression can no longer reference another instance member (`frameHub`) the way a plain class's implicit init allowed — `CameraSessionController.burstCapture` needed an explicit `init()` instead of `private(set) lazy var burstCapture = BurstCaptureManager(frameHub: frameHub)`. Not a functional loss: `frameHub` was already available at init time, so the laziness wasn't buying anything.
  - Replaced the Combine `zoomLock.$requestedZoomFactor.sink { ... }` subscription in `CameraViewModel` (used to mirror zoom into per-tab remembered state) with a plain closure property, `ZoomLockController.onRequestedZoomFactorChanged`, invoked from `requestedZoomFactor`'s existing `didSet` — `@Observable` types don't expose Combine's `$property` publishers the way `@Published` did.
